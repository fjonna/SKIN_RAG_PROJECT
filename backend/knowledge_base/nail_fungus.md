# Nail Fungus

## Overview
Nail fungus is a fungal infection that can make nails thick, discolored, brittle, or distorted.

## Skin Symptoms
Thickened nail, yellow nail, brown nail, brittle nail, crumbly nail, distorted nail, nail debris, and lifting nail.

## Visual Appearance
One or more nails may become yellow-brown, thick, crumbly, or separated from the nail bed.

## Distinguishing Features
The changes stay within the nail unit. Infection begins at the free edge or side corner and creeps backward toward the cuticle, piling crumbly debris beneath the plate and lifting the nail away from its bed. Unlike nail psoriasis there are no pinpoint pits on the surface and no oily yellow-brown patch showing through. Unlike a bruise under the nail the discoloration does not grow out and disappear.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Toenails above all, especially the great toe, and less often the fingernails; usually several nails over time. Common alongside athlete's foot, and more frequent with age, diabetes, poor circulation, occlusive footwear and communal barefoot areas.

## Possible Causes
Fungi can enter through small nail breaks and may spread from athlete foot or damp environments.

## Risk Groups
Older people and people with diabetes, circulation problems, sweaty footwear, nail injury, or reduced immunity.

## Risk Level
Usually low to moderate.

## Recommended Next Steps
Keep nails clean and dry and ask a clinician or pharmacist about treatment.

## Warning Signs
Seek care for painful, red, swollen, draining, rapidly changing, or persistent nail changes.
