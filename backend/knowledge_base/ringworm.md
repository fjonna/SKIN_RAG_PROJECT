# Ringworm

## Overview
Ringworm is a fungal skin infection, not a worm, that often causes an itchy, scaly patch with a noticeable outer edge.

## Skin Symptoms
Ring-shaped rash, circular scaly patch, raised red border, itchy patch, expanding rash, flaky skin, and central clearing.

## Visual Appearance
A circular scaly patch may have a raised border and clearer center.

## Distinguishing Features
The ring itself is the diagnosis: a round or oval patch with a raised, scaly, actively advancing rim and a centre that has already calmed and looks nearly normal. The patch expands outward steadily, enlarging week by week, and itches along the edge. Unlike eczema the outline is a clean circle with a defined border. Unlike psoriasis the middle clears instead of staying thickly scaled. It is the ring-shaped form of tinea on the body and belongs to the same family as other fungal skin infections. Despite the name no worm is involved.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Trunk, arms, legs, neck and other exposed skin, usually as one or a few separate patches rather than a widespread rash. Caught from infected people, shared towels and bedding, gym mats, and frequently from cats, dogs and farm animals.

## Possible Causes
Fungi can spread through infected people, animals, surfaces, or shared personal items.

## Risk Groups
People with close contact, shared sports equipment, communal changing-area exposure, animal contact, or moist skin.

## Risk Level
Usually low to moderate.

## Recommended Next Steps
Keep the area clean and dry and seek advice about antifungal treatment.

## Warning Signs
Seek care if it spreads, affects the scalp or face, is painful, infected, or does not improve.
