# Melanocytic Nevus

## Overview
A melanocytic nevus, commonly called a mole, is a cluster of pigment-producing cells that may be flat or raised and is often harmless.

## Skin Symptoms
Mole, brown spot, tan spot, dark skin spot, raised mole, flat mole, pigmented lesion, and changing mole.

## Visual Appearance
Moles may be flat or raised and vary in color from tan to dark brown.

## Distinguishing Features
The reassuring features are symmetry, a single even colour throughout, a smooth regular border, a size usually under six millimetres, and above all stability -- it has looked the same for years. That stability is what separates it from melanoma. A person's ordinary moles tend to resemble one another, so a benign mole fits the pattern of its neighbours rather than standing out from them. It does not bleed on its own, does not itch persistently and does not change quickly.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Anywhere on the body, most numerous on sun-exposed skin of the trunk and limbs. Most appear during childhood and early adult life, may darken slightly in pregnancy or with sun, and slowly fade in later decades.

## Possible Causes
Moles develop from pigment cells; inherited factors and sun exposure may influence their appearance.

## Risk Groups
People with many moles or a personal or family history of melanoma need regular skin checks.

## Risk Level
Usually low.

## Recommended Next Steps
Monitor for change and have concerning moles examined by a dermatologist.

## Warning Signs
Seek assessment for changes in asymmetry, border, color, size, itch, pain, or bleeding.
