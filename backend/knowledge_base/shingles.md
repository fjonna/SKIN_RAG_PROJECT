# Shingles

## Overview
Shingles occurs when the virus that causes chickenpox reactivates and often causes a painful band of grouped blisters on one side of the body.

## Skin Symptoms
Painful blister rash, burning skin, tingling skin, one-sided rash, grouped blisters, crusted blisters, and sensitive skin.

## Visual Appearance
Grouped blisters commonly appear in a band on one side of the body or face.

## Distinguishing Features
Two features settle it. First, the rash is strictly one-sided and stops abruptly at the midline, occupying a single band of skin served by one nerve. Second, burning, stabbing or tingling pain arrives days before any rash appears, and the skin may be too tender to bear clothing. Blisters come in tight clusters on a red base. Unlike chickenpox it is localised to one band rather than scattered over the whole body. Unlike cellulitis the dominant feature is nerve pain with blisters rather than spreading hot swelling. Pain can outlast the rash by months.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Most often a band around one side of the chest, back or waist; also one side of the face, and when it reaches the eye region it needs urgent assessment. Occurs in people who have previously had chickenpox, and is commoner with age and with reduced immunity.

## Possible Causes
Reactivation of varicella-zoster virus after prior chickenpox infection.

## Risk Groups
Older people and people with reduced immune function have a higher risk.

## Risk Level
Moderate to high.

## Recommended Next Steps
Contact a healthcare professional promptly for a new painful blistering rash.

## Warning Signs
Seek urgent care for rash near the eye or face, vision changes, severe pain, or widespread blisters.
