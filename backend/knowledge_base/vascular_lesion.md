# Vascular Lesion

## Overview
Vascular lesions are skin marks or growths involving blood vessels. Many are benign, but new or changing lesions need assessment.

## Skin Symptoms
Red spot, purple spot, vascular bump, red papule, bleeding lesion, blanching lesion, and clustered red vessels.

## Visual Appearance
Small red or purple spots, raised bumps, or visible vessels may occur.

## Distinguishing Features
These lesions are built from blood vessels, so the colour is red, purple or blue rather than brown or black, and many blanch briefly when pressed with a glass slide. Cherry angiomas are small, bright red, smooth domed spots that accumulate with age. Spider veins show a central point with radiating threads. Port-wine stains are flat and present from birth. Unlike melanoma the colour is never brown or black. Unlike a wart the surface is smooth rather than rough.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Trunk and upper arms for cherry angiomas; the face, nose and cheeks for spider veins and thread veins; the legs for larger dilated vessels. Most are harmless and stable, though they may bleed briskly if knocked or scratched.

## Possible Causes
Vascular lesions can be congenital or develop as blood vessels change with age, hormones, injury, or other skin changes.

## Risk Groups
They can occur at any age; some types are more common in infancy, pregnancy, or adulthood.

## Risk Level
Usually low to moderate.

## Recommended Next Steps
Avoid picking and have a changing or bleeding lesion assessed.

## Warning Signs
Seek care for a new, rapidly growing, painful, repeatedly bleeding, ulcerated, or color-changing lesion.
