# Chickenpox

## Overview
Chickenpox is a contagious viral illness that usually causes crops of itchy spots and fluid-filled blisters that later crust.

## Skin Symptoms
Itchy blister rash, small fluid-filled blisters, red spots, crusted spots, widespread rash, and scratched lesions.

## Visual Appearance
Red spots, blisters, and crusted spots can occur at different stages together.

## Distinguishing Features
The hallmark is lesions at several different stages present at the same moment: flat red spots, raised bumps, clear fluid-filled blisters and crusted scabs all together in one area. Fever, headache and malaise usually precede the rash by a day or two. Unlike eczema it is acute, self-limiting over one to two weeks and accompanied by fever. Unlike shingles it is widespread and on both sides of the body rather than confined to a single band. It is highly contagious until every lesion has crusted.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Starts on the trunk, face and scalp and spreads outward to the limbs, denser centrally than peripherally. Involves the scalp and the inside of the mouth, which most other itchy rashes spare. Mainly affects children, and adults more severely.

## Possible Causes
Varicella-zoster virus causes chickenpox and can spread to people without immunity.

## Risk Groups
Infants, adults, pregnant people, and people with weakened immune systems have higher complication risk.

## Risk Level
Moderate.

## Recommended Next Steps
Follow clinical and public-health advice and avoid scratching.

## Warning Signs
Seek prompt care for severe rash, skin infection signs, breathing difficulty, confusion, or high-risk exposure.
