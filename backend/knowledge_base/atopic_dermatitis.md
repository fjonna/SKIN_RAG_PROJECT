# Atopic Dermatitis

## Overview
Atopic dermatitis is a common form of eczema with a sensitive, inflamed skin barrier and recurrent itchy, dry patches.

## Skin Symptoms
Intense itching, dry skin, red rash, scaly patches, cracked or thickened skin, inflammation, and weeping skin.

## Visual Appearance
Dry, red, scaly, or cracked patches may occur in recurring flares.

## Distinguishing Features
A chronic relapsing condition that begins in childhood and sits in the skin folds, usually with a personal or family history of asthma or hay fever. Unlike contact eczema it is not limited to the area something touched. Unlike psoriasis the scale is fine rather than thick and silvery, the border is indistinct rather than sharply cut, and the itch is far more intense. Repeated scratching thickens the skin into leathery lichenified patches.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Bends of the elbows and behind the knees above all, plus the neck, wrists, ankles and eyelids. In infants it favours the cheeks, scalp and the outer surfaces of the limbs. Flares follow dryness, heat, soap, wool, stress and seasonal change.

## Possible Causes
Skin-barrier and immune factors, irritants, allergens, climate changes, and scratching can contribute.

## Risk Groups
Children and people with a personal or family history of allergic conditions.

## Risk Level
Usually low to moderate.

## Recommended Next Steps
Use fragrance-free moisturizers and avoid personal irritants.

## Warning Signs
Seek care for pus, yellow crusting, painful cracking, rapid spread, or persistent flares.
