# Fungal Infection

## Overview
Fungal skin infections can cause itchy, scaly, peeling, or ring-shaped patches.

## Skin Symptoms
Itchy rash, scaly patches, peeling skin, red border, circular rash, flaky skin, cracked skin, and irritation.

## Visual Appearance
Patches may have scaling and a more noticeable red outer edge.

## Distinguishing Features
The umbrella term for tinea and yeast infections of the skin, covering the groin, feet, scalp, nails and body folds. The shared signature is an active, advancing scaly edge with skin that looks calmer behind it, plus prominent itch. Unlike eczema the border is raised and clearly drawn rather than fading gradually. Unlike psoriasis the scale sits at the rim rather than covering a thick plaque, and the centre tends to clear. Note that ringworm is the specific ring-shaped form of this same family of infections on the body.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Warm, moist, occluded sites: groin and inner thighs, under the breasts, armpits, between the toes, and the trunk or limbs. Spread by damp clothing, shared towels, gym surfaces and contact with infected animals.

## Possible Causes
Fungi can spread through contact, shared items, moisture, and warm occluded skin.

## Risk Groups
People with moist skin, shared changing-area exposure, diabetes, skin injury, or reduced immunity.

## Risk Level
Usually low to moderate.

## Recommended Next Steps
Keep skin clean and dry and seek advice about appropriate antifungal care.

## Warning Signs
Seek care for spreading, painful, infected, facial, scalp, or persistent rashes.
