# Cellulitis

## Overview
Cellulitis is a bacterial infection of deeper skin layers that needs timely clinical evaluation.

## Skin Symptoms
Spreading redness, warm skin, swelling, tenderness, pain, skin tightness, and a red swollen area.

## Visual Appearance
An area of skin may become increasingly red, warm, swollen, and tender.

## Distinguishing Features
This is an acute bacterial infection of the deeper skin, and the person feels unwell with it -- fever, chills and malaise accompany the rash. The skin is hot, tense, painful to touch and the redness has an indistinct spreading edge that advances over hours. Unlike eczema the dominant symptom is pain rather than itch. Unlike contact dermatitis it expands within hours rather than days and is almost always one-sided. Rapid spread, fever or a marked line of advance needs urgent care.

## Typical Location and Pattern
The lower leg is by far the commonest site, usually one leg only; also the forearm, hand and the skin around the eye. Often follows a break in the skin -- a cut, bite, ulcer, or cracked skin between the toes from athlete's foot.

## Possible Causes
Bacteria can enter through a cut, ulcer, bite, puncture, or other break in the skin.

## Risk Groups
People with broken skin, diabetes, circulation problems, chronic skin conditions, or reduced immune function.

## Risk Level
High.

## Recommended Next Steps
Seek prompt medical assessment.

## Warning Signs
Urgently seek care for rapidly spreading redness, severe pain, blistering, or signs of systemic illness.
