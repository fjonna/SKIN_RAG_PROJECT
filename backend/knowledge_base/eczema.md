# Eczema

## Overview
Eczema is an inflammatory skin condition that can make skin dry, itchy, irritated, and prone to cracking.

## Skin Symptoms
Dry skin, itchy rash, red patches, rough skin, scaling, cracks, irritation, and weeping eczema.

## Visual Appearance
Dry, red, scaly, rough, or cracked patches may occur.

## Distinguishing Features
A general term for inflamed, itchy, barrier-damaged skin. When a contact trigger is responsible, the rash is sharply limited to exactly the area the substance touched, which no other condition mimics. Hand eczema follows repeated wet work, detergents and solvents. Unlike atopic dermatitis it can begin in adulthood, need not sit in the skin folds and need not come with asthma or hay fever. Unlike a fungal infection there is no advancing scaly ring and no clearing centre.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Hands, fingers, finger webs and wrists most often, and otherwise wherever the trigger made contact -- under jewellery, along a watch strap, at a glove cuff. Small deep intensely itchy blisters along the sides of the fingers are a distinct pattern.

## Possible Causes
Skin-barrier sensitivity, irritants, allergens, dry air, and scratching can contribute to flares.

## Risk Groups
People of any age, especially those with a personal or family history of allergic conditions.

## Risk Level
Usually low to moderate.

## Recommended Next Steps
Use regular bland moisturizers and reduce exposure to personal irritants.

## Warning Signs
Seek care for pain, pus, yellow crusting, rapid spread, or persistent symptoms.
