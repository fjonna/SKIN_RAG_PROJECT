# Impetigo

## Overview
Impetigo is a contagious superficial bacterial skin infection that can form sores or blisters followed by yellow crusts.

## Skin Symptoms
Honey-colored crust, yellow crust, red sores, blisters, oozing lesions, crusted rash, and facial sores.

## Visual Appearance
Small red lesions can break and develop a golden or honey-colored crust.

## Distinguishing Features
The golden, honey-coloured crust is the single most recognisable feature and effectively settles the diagnosis. Thin-walled blisters rupture within hours and dry into that sticky yellow-brown scab. It is highly contagious and spreads between children and across the patient's own skin by scratching. Unlike eczema the main finding is crusting rather than dryness and itch. Unlike cold sores the blisters are not tightly grouped on a red base and do not recur in the same spot.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Around the nose and mouth above all, plus the chin, cheeks, hands, forearms and legs. Often starts where the skin was already broken by a scratch, insect bite, cut or eczema patch. Mainly affects young children, especially in warm weather and close quarters.

## Possible Causes
Bacteria can infect broken skin and spread through contact or scratching.

## Risk Groups
Children and people with skin injury, eczema, close contact, or crowded settings.

## Risk Level
Moderate.

## Recommended Next Steps
Avoid sharing towels and seek clinical advice about treatment.

## Warning Signs
Seek prompt care for spreading lesions, painful swelling, eye involvement, or persistent symptoms.
