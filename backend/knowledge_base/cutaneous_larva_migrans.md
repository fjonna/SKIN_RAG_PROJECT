# Cutaneous Larva Migrans

## Overview
Cutaneous larva migrans is a parasitic skin infection caused by hookworm larvae that penetrate the skin, typically after contact with contaminated soil or sand.

## Skin Symptoms
Intensely itchy skin, a raised winding or serpiginous track, redness along the track, mild swelling, and occasional blistering.

## Visual Appearance
A thin, raised, red-brown line that advances slowly across the skin over days, often on the feet, lower legs, buttocks, or hands.

## Distinguishing Features
The one skin condition that visibly moves: the raised winding track advances several millimetres to a few centimetres each day, so its position changes from one day to the next. Nothing else in dermatology migrates like this. The itch is severe and often worse at night. It follows skin contact with sand or soil contaminated by animal faeces.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Feet and soles above all, plus the buttocks, thighs and hands -- whichever skin rested directly on contaminated sand or earth. Typically follows barefoot beach exposure or travel in tropical and subtropical regions.

## Possible Causes
Hookworm larvae, commonly from dog or cat feces contaminating soil or sand, burrow into skin on contact, most often through bare feet.

## Risk Groups
People who walk barefoot on beaches or soil in tropical and subtropical regions, and travelers returning from such areas.

## Risk Level
Usually low.

## Recommended Next Steps
Avoid scratching and seek medical evaluation for antiparasitic treatment to relieve symptoms and prevent secondary infection.

## Warning Signs
Seek care for signs of secondary bacterial infection such as spreading redness, pus, fever, or worsening pain.
