# Melanoma

## Overview
Melanoma is a potentially serious skin cancer that can develop in an existing mole or as a new pigmented lesion.

## Skin Symptoms
Asymmetric mole, irregular border, multiple colors, dark changing spot, enlarging mole, bleeding mole, and evolving lesion.

## Visual Appearance
A mole or spot may be asymmetric, irregularly bordered, multicolored, or changing.

## Distinguishing Features
The warning pattern is summarised as ABCDE: Asymmetry between the two halves, Border that is ragged or notched, Colour that varies with browns, blacks, reds or greys in one lesion, Diameter beyond about six millimetres, and Evolution -- any change in size, shape, colour, or new bleeding or itching. A second clue is the ugly duckling sign: the lesion stands out as different from all the person's other moles. Unlike a benign mole it changes over months. Unlike seborrheic keratosis it is not waxy or stuck-on. Unlike a vascular lesion the colour is brown or black rather than red or purple.

## Typical Location and Pattern
The back is the commonest site in men and the lower legs in women, but it can arise anywhere, including the soles, palms, under a nail, the scalp and rarely areas never exposed to sun. It may develop in an existing mole or on previously clear skin.

## Possible Causes
Ultraviolet exposure and genetic factors contribute to melanoma risk.

## Risk Groups
People with many or atypical moles, family history, intense ultraviolet exposure, or reduced immune function.

## Risk Level
High.

## Recommended Next Steps
Seek prompt dermatologist assessment for a suspicious or changing lesion.

## Warning Signs
Urgently assess a new or evolving dark lesion, especially if it bleeds or changes rapidly.
