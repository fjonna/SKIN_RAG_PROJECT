# Basal Cell Carcinoma

## Overview
Basal cell carcinoma is a common skin cancer that may resemble a shiny bump, nonhealing sore, or scar-like patch.

## Skin Symptoms
Pearly bump, shiny nodule, nonhealing sore, bleeding spot, crusted lesion, pink growth, or scar-like patch.

## Visual Appearance
A translucent or pink growth may repeatedly crust, bleed, or fail to heal.

## Distinguishing Features
The defining look is a pearly, translucent papule with a rolled, raised edge and fine branching blood vessels running across its surface. It bleeds after trivial contact, scabs over, then breaks down again -- a sore that never finishes healing over months. Unlike a wart it is smooth rather than rough and verrucous. Unlike seborrheic keratosis it is not waxy, brown or stuck-on. Unlike a cherry angioma the vessels lie within a firm pearly nodule rather than forming the whole lesion. It grows slowly and rarely spreads, but destroys local tissue.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Sun-exposed skin of the head and neck above all: nose, nasal folds, cheeks, forehead, eyelids, ears and scalp, and also the upper trunk and shoulders. Most common in older adults with fair skin and years of cumulative sun exposure.

## Possible Causes
Ultraviolet damage from sun exposure or indoor tanning is a major risk factor.

## Risk Groups
People with long-term ultraviolet exposure, lighter skin, older age, prior skin cancer, or reduced immune function.

## Risk Level
High.

## Recommended Next Steps
Arrange a dermatologist assessment for any suspicious growth.

## Warning Signs
Seek prompt care for a new, changing, bleeding, crusted, or nonhealing lesion.
