# Benign Keratosis

## Overview
Benign keratoses are noncancerous growths that can look waxy, rough, or stuck onto skin; visual appearance alone cannot confirm this.

## Skin Symptoms
Waxy growth, raised rough spot, stuck-on lesion, tan or brown patch, scaly growth, and crusty plaque.

## Visual Appearance
Often a waxy, rough, raised brown or tan growth.

## Distinguishing Features
The hallmark is a stuck-on appearance, as though the growth were pasted onto the skin rather than growing out of it, with a waxy or greasy surface and a sharply cut edge. Unlike a viral wart it lacks the rough papillomatous surface and the tiny black dots of thrombosed capillaries. Unlike melanoma the border is regular and sharply defined and the surface waxy, and it does not bleed on its own or change quickly. Numbers increase steadily with age.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Trunk above all -- back and chest -- plus the face, scalp, neck and skin folds. Lesions are usually multiple, appear from middle age onward and enlarge extremely slowly over years without any tendency to ulcerate.

## Possible Causes
These growths are common with aging and may have a genetic contribution.

## Risk Groups
Adults and people with a family history of similar growths.

## Risk Level
Usually low.

## Recommended Next Steps
Avoid picking and have uncertain lesions examined.

## Warning Signs
Seek care if a lesion changes, bleeds, becomes painful, or looks unlike other spots.
