# Warts and Molluscum

## Overview
Warts and molluscum are common viral skin conditions that cause small raised bumps. Warts are often rough, while molluscum may have a central indentation.

## Skin Symptoms
Rough skin bump, wart, skin-colored papules, umbilicated bumps, clustered bumps, raised lesion, and itchy bumps.

## Visual Appearance
Warts may be rough; molluscum bumps are often smooth with a central dimple.

## Distinguishing Features
Two different viral bumps with opposite surfaces. Warts are rough, firm and cauliflower-like, interrupt the normal skin lines, and show tiny black dots -- clotted capillaries -- within the surface. Molluscum bumps are the reverse: smooth, pearly, dome-shaped and firm, each with a small central dimple or pit. Unlike seborrheic keratosis both are viral, occur in children and young adults, and spread by contact and by scratching to new sites. Unlike basal cell carcinoma neither is pearly with branching vessels and neither ulcerates and fails to heal.

## Typical Location and Pattern
Warts favour the fingers, backs of the hands, around the nails, knees and the soles, where pressure drives them inward and makes walking painful. Molluscum favours the trunk, armpits, sides of the chest, face and the bends of the limbs in children, often in a line where scratching spread them.

## Possible Causes
Viruses can spread through direct contact, shared items, or scratching.

## Risk Groups
Children, people with eczema, close-contact groups, and people with reduced immune function.

## Risk Level
Usually low.

## Recommended Next Steps
Avoid picking or shaving over bumps and seek advice if diagnosis or treatment is needed.

## Warning Signs
Seek care for painful, bleeding, infected, rapidly spreading, facial, genital, or persistent bumps.
